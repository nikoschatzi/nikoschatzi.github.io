Rad-hard local electronics

As part of the HL-LHC upgrade, piezo valve actuators may be installed in areas exposed to radiation levels exceeding 50 kGy. The actuators originally used integrated industrial electronics containing ICs and microcontrollers. These components can fail at radiation doses significantly below the expected environmental exposure, making the original electronics unsuitable for operation in highly radiated areas.

To address this limitation, a custom CERN PCB was developed to replace the integrated electronics while maintaining the original actuator functionality. The design deliberately avoided ICs and microcontrollers, using only relays, resistors, capacitors, and diodes to provide a simplified and more radiation-tolerant implementation.

The original electronics had to be carefully removed from the valve actuators before installing the CERN PCB. After modification, the actuators were tested to verify that the replacement electronics preserved the required operating functionality.

The radiation environment also affected the mechanical components surrounding the electronics. In a passive radiation test, a piezo valve actuator was exposed to 56 kGy to evaluate the effects of radiation on its materials. Following irradiation, the original black valve holding case became brittle.

To address this issue, the valve case was redesigned and 3D printed using Accura25 and ULTEM materials. These replacement cases were intended for further radiation testing to evaluate their long-term mechanical performance under the expected operating conditions.

The resulting solution combines simplified radiation-tolerant electronics with redesigned mechanical components, providing a path for operating the piezo valve actuators in the high-radiation areas foreseen for the HL-LHC upgrade.
